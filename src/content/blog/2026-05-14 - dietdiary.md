## Background

Back in December 2025, I was finally diagnosed with ADHD, after a few aborted attempts (and in particular an unsuccessful autism assessment when I was 17).

I've always been a picky eater, both for texture and taste reasons, but I've never taken much care to do anything about it for a long time. In addition, I've always struggled with pica, and in particular will eat paper, rubber, and plastic if it's left within easy reach.

In recent months and more generally over the past year, I've been trying to find more concrete ways to increase fibre in my diet in particular.

The journey is less about the absolute best practice or the most balanced diet, etc. It's truly about trying to find the solutions that are sustainable even if they're not the ABSOLUTE best.

So here are some things I've tried that have stuck. They're not all directly applicable to everyone, of course, but they're things that I have been able to try at least.

## Why is this post about snacks? Surely _meals_ matter more?
This may not be a question _you_ specifically were asking, but it IS something that _I_ have been sitting with for a while.

Ultimately, it comes down to the fact that I have always found snacking to be both a necessary thing to do, and a hard thing for me to change my habits in.

Snacking is so necessary to me _because_ of the ADHD. My sleep pattern is shot to hell, and because I am my mum's carer, I often find myself needing to use what few spoons I am afforded on preparing food for her rather than myself.

It's all about finding ways to redirect my snacking such that if they're not """"healthy"""" (for some value of that word), they are at least achieving my nutrition goals as best I am physically able.

If that's the best I can do, it is at least better than nothing. (#ADHDAffirmations)

## Change #1: Access to snacks

For Christmas 2025, my parents got me a mini fridge. It's not a particularly great one, but the key is that it's silent (and thus okay to be in my bedroom) and is good ENOUGH to store drinks, stable snacks (like chocolate bars), and condiments in.

As a result, I now keep my bedroom better-stocked with a range of snacks I can easily reach for if I need to, whether that's because I don't feel like going downstairs, or because I forgot to eat for too long and need to get my blood sugar up fast.

The general contents of this fridge most of the time:

- Drinks: <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Old Jamaica_ ginger beer</span>, multiple large (refillable) bottles of water
- Chocolate: Whatever I feel like. Currently there are a bunch of <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Lidl Mister Choc Milk & Hazelnut Bars_</span> in there.
- Yoghurts: <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Lidl Milbona Breakfast Pots: Raspberry & Blackcurrant_</span>.
- Condiments: ketchup, mayonnaise, mustard
- Breakfast shake: <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Huel Powder Chocolate_</span>

I also keep next to my chair a box that has my ambient snacks. Mostly, this is crisps, crackers, biscuits (currently <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Lidl Arctic_</span> bars (own-brand _Penguins_)), and sweets. But this also includes a bunch of cereal bars (<span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Kellogg's Special K_</span>, both milk and dark chocolate varieties).

## Change #2: Battling pica (and executive dysfunction)

To be perfectly honest, the thing that has MOST helped my pica has been starting medication for my ADHD. And I don't know if that's a reduced anxiety and lethargy thing, or if it's just a side-effect of the appetite suppression.

However, even before I recently started meds, I was making one change that was at least reducing the frequency of my pica: keeping <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>chewing gum</span> *constantly* stocked and within reach.

For me, this generally means that I have at least one bottle of chewing gum next to my chair, AND a bottle that is designated to be put into my bag when I'm going out.

This also serves another purpose: I quite often don't have the executive function to actually brush my teeth, but at least access to chewing gum (and especially whitening/cleaning gum) means I can get food out of my teeth and often remove at least some plaque.

This may not be a viable option for some people, particularly those who are sensitive to sugar alcohols (polyols).

## Change #3: Drinks

For around a decade, I had a pretty heavy addiction to <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Pepsi_</span>, until some time in around 2018-2019 I couldn't afford to buy any one month. After trying to start drinking it again, I realised that I physically cannot handle caffeine anymore, and so was suddenly stuck for what to do for drinks.

This was around the time that almost every soft drink in the UK had just reformulated itself to reduce sugar to below 4.5g/100ml. The issue is that I DO NOT like the taste of artificial sweeteners, and in particular aspartame and acesulfame K. I was also living in an area of the country with MUCH softer water than I am used to, so drinking tap water was a difficult ask too.

In the end, I discovered that citrus drinks masked the flavour of the artificial sweeteners well, and I moved onto first <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Tango_</span> and later <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Fanta_</span>. But I would be getting through 2-litre bottles of the stuff in the span of a day to a day and a half. Hardly sustainable, or cheap.

In 2025, trying to reduce my sugar intake further, I finally got myself drinking water more consistently, but still had issues going to refill my drinks constantly. I also swapped the remaining _Fanta_ in my diet for <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Old Jamaica Ginger Beer_</span>, which happened to be another drink I found masked the taste of the artificial sweeteners quite well.

Finally, once I got my snack fridge, I found a regime that works for me, and maybe it would work for others too. I have one 700ml drinks bottle that I drink water out of. But I also have other bottles that are harder to drink from for one reason or another. I keep those other bottles filled and store them in my fridge, ready to decant into my drinking bottle when it gets empty.

Then once every day or two, depending on how much I'm drinking at the time, I can take all the bottles downstairs at once to refill them, or I can take the empty bottles down to refill as and when I remember. The key is that this strategy is adaptable to whatever my ADHD is doing that day.

On top of the water, I generally have one can (330 ml) of _Old Jamaica_ a day, on average. A far cry from the 1.5-2 litres of _Fanta_ or _Pepsi_ in days gone by.

## Change #4: The actual snacks

So as I said, it was a bunch of individual substitutions and finding specific snacks that helped a lot. Not all of these suggestions will work for you, and I'm not even suggesting that any individual one will be directly applicable, but I hope it at least gives you some ideas of where to start.

Most of this is from Lidl because that's where we shop the most, and especially where I shop the most in-person.

Almost all of my day-to-day snacking currently is <span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian" style="color:#eebb44"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i>vegetarian</span>. Some are <span role="img" aria-label="vegan" style="color:#18c638"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i>vegan</span>.

### Chocolate bars
I personally don't think it's realistic, at least for me, to say "Just don't buy chocolate". The impulse for me manifests itself no matter how hard I try to push back against it.

The thing that HAS helped for me is finding chocolate and sweets that also fulfill ANOTHER diet goal.

For this reason, Lidl's <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Mister Choc Milk & Hazelnuts Bars_</span> have been helpful. (These are just Lidl's own-brand version of <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Kinder Bueno_</span>. They're not THAT high in fibre, but they are 2.6% fibre, as well as containing 11% hazelnut.

Nuts don't count as a fruit or vegetable under UK health guidance (even though pulses do, but that's another conversation), but they are generally a good small way to balance types of fat, and to get a range of nutrients. Unfortunately, I REALLY do not like the texture of most nuts. Chopped hazelnuts or cashews IN things are about the best I can do.

### Fruit snacks
I've replaced a lot of my day-to-day sweets snacking with Lidl's <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Alesto Mango & Passion Fruit Snacking Fruit with Cashew Nuts_</span>. (Catchy!)

These are in basically the same boat as the hazelnut chocolate, though they do have the significant advantage of ACTUALLY containing enough fruit to count under UK health guidelines.

They're again not amazingly high in fibre, as they're mostly made from juice rather than pulp. For this reason I'd consider them to be "fruit juice" rather than dried fruit for 5-a-day purposes (i.e., only one serving 'counts').

And again, the cashews help with both fibre and fat goals, as well as replacing some carbs with protein instead.

### Cereal bars
The core of my snacking arsenal these days is <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Kellogg's Special K_ cereal bars</span>.
I've tried all three generally available varieties, but I really don't rate the berry one. It's just too sweet and has an almost artificial 'red berry' flavour to it that's hard to describe.

Between the milk and dark chocolate, it really comes down to personal preference. I like them both. The milk is obviously sweeter, of course, but I don't mind the lucky dip of which I get.

Unfortunately, even the dark chocolate and berries varieties have milk in them so they are <span role="img" aria-label="not vegan" style="color:#ff6688"><i class="ti ti-leaf-off"></i>not&nbsp;vegan</span>.

<span role="img" aria-label="vegan" style="color:#18c638"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i>Vegan</span> cereal bars obviously DO exist, they just were not a priority to me in my endeavours.

The key to getting to this stage though was a LOT of trial and error. I bought one box of almost every cereal bar I could find that didn't have something about it that was an outright rejection. For me, I managed to eliminate a lot of stuff just on the basis of being peanut or cashew-based.

I also tried and rated quite highly <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Cadbury's Brunch Chunky Choc Chips_ bars</span>.

### Yoghurts
This was the big game changer for me, to be quite honest.

As I've mentioned a few times now, nuts and seeds are a challenge for me. I just do not like the flavour of some nuts, and the texture generally of both nuts and seeds.

I've never done well with things that are too grainy or bitty. For similar reasons, I am iffy about beans, picky about chickpeas, and I don't like hard corn products either (tortilla chips or even really popcorn). I have a similar problem with porridges, though they are generally a bit easier.

But in January 2026 &ndash; when I was already in the middle of trying ways to have porridges, yoghurts, and fromage frais as snacks and breakfasts &ndash; Lidl started selling <span style="color:#eebb44"><span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Milbona Breakfast Pots_</span>, which are 150g pots of yoghurt with a fruit compote and mixed seeds and oats in them.

They come in two flavours. One is something like _Passion Fruit and Coconut_, but I do not like coconut. The other, however, is _Raspberry and Blackcurrant_ and I DO like both of those.

The _Raspberry and Blackcurrant_ variety is definitely sweet, especially once fully mixed in, but that sweetness comes from oligosaccharides moreso than sugar, and the flavour is very distinctly from the yoghurt and the fruit compote, not from the added carbs.

At 7.5g of fibre per pot, they are VERY high in fibre for a single serving of something that isn't just... a salad. A lot of that fibre comes from the seeds and grains, but I will acknowledge that some of it is added fibre in the form of oligofructose. Still, this means that as well as the general benefits of yoghurt, there is a good mix of types of fibre and some prebiotic also.

Now obviously, you _could_ make something like this yourself. But this is my point. In practice, I would do it for a short while, and then get tired of doing it and forget about it. In fact, that was already happening with my porridge/yoghurt experiments just before I discovered these pots.

Also, as these are pre-packaged as a single item they generally have long enough dates that I can keep them in my fridge for a while. I wouldn't feel comfortable keeping a prepared tub of yoghurt+oats+seeds in my snack fridge with its slightly-less-than-ideal temperature control, and keeping it in the main fridge would defeat the point in the first place.

## Change #5: Breakfast
I never used to have breakfast. In fact, I tended to find that if I had breakfast that meant I would just start eating and not stop for the rest of the day, and that was not a sustainable model for me.

However, my hand was kind of forced on this one by going onto meds for my ADHD. I need to have breakfast before they kick in, because they are an appetite suppressant.

Luckily, it just so happened that immediately before I started on the meds, we had picked up some <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Huel_ products</span> to try out for unrelated reasons.

Both my mum and I turned out to like <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Huel Powder_</span>, particularly the chocolate flavour, so that became my breakfast.

But it's relevant here because it's another thing I feel comfortable storing in my snack fridge. I make it in the afternoons or evenings, whenever I get chance (usually around the time I'm making dinner for myself and my mum), and then can store it in the fridge ready to grab immediately the next morning to take my meds with. It's high-protein, nutritionally well-balanced, and crucially it means that even if I don't feel like making another meal that day, I've at least had something with a good balance of macro and micronutrients at the start of the day.

## The change that didn't work: protein snacks
It's also worth mentioning that I did try a variety of options for replacing carbs with protein in my snacking that just did not work out for a variety of reasons.

Firstly, <span style="color:#ff6688"><span role="img" aria-label="not vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf-off"></i></span>snacking salamis</span> were something I tried for a while, but they are generally VERY high in both saturated fat AND salt, and I found that they probably weren't a sustainable switch long-term.

Secondly, I occasionally get <span style="color:#ff6688"><span role="img" aria-label="not vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf-off"></i></span>_Mr Porky Crispy Strips_</span> or other pork crunch/pork scratchings products. However, _Crispy Strips_ are expensive per pack, and if I have them around too long I find it hard to not eat them all at once. Meanwhile, other pork crunch products are generally WAY too salty, and pork scratchings are too inconsistent and I really don't like the overdone and stale-tasting bits you often get in them.

Finally, I tried a variety of commercial protein bars and "protein biscuits", etc. (<span role="img" aria-label="vegan" style="color:#18c638"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i>vegan</span>, <span role="img" aria-label="vegetarian" style="color:#eebb44"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i>vegetarian</span>, _and_ <span role="img" aria-label="not vegetarian" style="color:#ff6688"><i class="ti ti-leaf-off"></i>not&nbsp;vegetarian</span>), but I always found them way too stodgy. In terms of a recommendation, my favourites were <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Huel Complete Nutrition Bars_</span>, which are also low in carbohydrates because they use stevia derivatives instead of sugar. However, I know stevia isn't for everyone, and _Huel_ and other meal replacement products are an acquired taste to some people.

That said, just this week I discovered and tried <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_La Vie Salami Sticks_</span> and <span style="color:#18c638"><span role="img" aria-label="vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf"></i></span>_Squeaky Bean Crispy Bacon Style Strips_</span> and found them both to be actually quite good. If you're looking to swap out <span style="color:#ff6688"><span role="img" aria-label="not vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf-off"></i></span>meat sticks</span> or <span style="color:#ff6688"><span role="img" aria-label="not vegan"><i class="ti ti-leaf-off"></i></span>crispy bacon</span> for a vegan alternative, these are worth a try. Just be aware that they are generally equally high in saturated fat and salt as the meat versions, if not more. They are also quite pricy, especially compared to, for instance, Lidl _Dulano_ meat products.

## Conclusion

Ultimately, I don't know if any of this will be helpful to anyone, or even if anyone will actually read it.

I just felt like it was necessary to get some of these thoughts out there into the world, and to reflect on the progress I have made, even if my diet is far from "ideal".
